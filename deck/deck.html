Closet Door

A camera on the closet door that reads each garment you hang on it — cutout, type, colour — and suggests what to wear today. On the device. Photos never leave home.

Ya-Cheng Chu · National Chung Hsing University, Department of Information Management

github.com/Chen931222/closet-door

01 · ProblemWhere this comes from

I photographed all 104 of my clothes on one door handle.
Doing it with a phone went wrong three ways.

1It is private.

A wardrobe is a picture of your body, your home and what you spend. Cloud vision APIs see all of it.

2Colour moves.

Under warm room light, light-blue jeans came out olive in this prototype. Every phone shot has different light.

3Nobody keeps it up.

One photo per item, upload, tag. The list is out of date the week after you buy something.

The door is already where clothes hang. If the camera is there too, adding an item is just hanging it up.

02 · SolutionWhat the device does

Hang it on the door. The closet updates itself.

Trigger · Stage IIA new object is hanging (frame difference); today: pick a photo
CutoutGarment mask, background removed
Type20 garment types → top / outer / bottom / shoes / bag
ColourMain colour, named; white-card calibration in Stage II
IndexStored on the device with its embedding
"You already own this."

Before the tag comes off: the closest items you have in the same slot, by image similarity. The demo shirt matches itself at 0.93.

"Wear this today."

Outfits that include the new item, ranked by warmth for today's temperature and two colour rules. Each suggestion prints its reasons.

03 · ArchitectureHardware and data flow

Everything that sees an image runs on the Hailo-10H.

PartRole
USB camera, top of the door frameFixed view, fixed light
ASUS UGen300 (Hailo-10H, 40 TOPS INT4, 8 GB)Cutout + image encoder
Small host PC / SBCTrigger, colour, index, rules, local web page
Phone on home Wi-FiReads the page; nothing is uploaded
Printed white card on the doorColour reference in every frame
What is not on the device
  • No cloud vision API.
  • No text encoder: the 20 garment prompts are embedded once at build time and shipped as a 20×512 table.
  • No account. The closet index is two local files.
04 · MethodsModels and tools in the Stage I prototype

Small, public models, all in ONNX — ready for the Hailo Dataflow Compiler.

StageModel / methodSizeLicence
CutoutU2Net-p, 320×320 salient-object mask4.6 MBApache-2.0
TypeCLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder, int8, zero-shot over 20 prompts ("a photo of a …", shoes "a pair of …")87 MBMIT
Colourk-means (k=3) on masked pixels → nearest of 19 named colours—own code
Already own itCosine similarity of CLIP embeddings, same slot—own code
OutfitsRules: warmth vs temperature, ≤1 accent colour, light/dark contrast—own code

Runtime today: ONNX Runtime on a laptop CPU, as a stand-in for the Hailo-10H. Python, FastAPI, numpy. Written with Claude Code as a coding assistant.

08 · LimitsFound while building Stage I

What does not work yet.

  1. Hanging shorts read as shirts in 2 of the first 8 raw door photos I checked by eye.
  2. Light colours under warm light are wrong: light-blue jeans → "olive"; light-gray shorts on a beige bed → "beige".
  3. The cutout keeps the door handle and hanger — and a dark box some older closet images carry, so a few closet shoes read "black".
  4. The outfit rules are mine and have not been tested on anyone else.
  5. Nothing has run on Hailo yet. Whether both graphs compile unchanged is unknown until Stage II.

10 · ValueWho would use it, and why on the edge

A closet that keeps itself up to date, without a cloud.

For people

Know what you own before you buy a near-duplicate; get an outfit for today's weather from clothes you actually have.

Why edge AI

Images of a bedroom and a body stay home. A fixed camera with fixed light gives colour a phone can't. No per-photo API cost.

Where it goes next

The same door camera fits shop fitting rooms and second-hand sellers listing many items — to be tested, not claimed.

Evidence so far is one closet — mine. Stage II adds a week of daily use; anything beyond that is a hypothesis.
